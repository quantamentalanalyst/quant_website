date: "2026-09-16"
abstract: "Everyone agrees the AI trade is crowded; almost nobody measures it. From 22,139 10-K filings I build a point-in-time AI-exposure score for the 1,000 largest US filers and ask two questions that can fail: is AI a factor, and is it a crowd? Disclosure went from 3% of filings mentioning AI in 2015 to 91% in 2026, but disclosure is not economics: pre-ChatGPT exposure did not predict the 2023 repricing (−0.3pp per σ, t = −0.3). As a factor, AI is mostly a bundle of known styles: long market, short size, value, profitability and conservative investment (R² 54%; 70% after ChatGPT), with no significant alpha (+3.5%/yr, t = 1.3) and no structural break. The Nov-2022 AI cohort's gain was 59% re-rating (41 of 69 log points), and 72% of its dollar gain came from five names. As a crowd, the evidence is specific: the AI leg holds 44% of universe float-cap (95th percentile), factor volatility is at its sample high, and since spring 2026 the top-decile core has comoved beyond size-matched peers (+0.043, CI [0.019, 0.071]). The valuation spread, by contrast, is unremarkable. None of these measures robustly predicts factor returns in 127 months; nothing survives Bonferroni. A century of survivorship-free industry history supplies the missing power: 100%+ two-year run-ups (raw and net of market) crash 40% within two years 26% of the time (CI 14–39) against a 7.9% base rate, and 46% of the time above 150%, without predictably low average returns. Semiconductors peaked at a 156% run-up (97% net, a hair under the threshold); Computers are inside a qualifying episode today."
