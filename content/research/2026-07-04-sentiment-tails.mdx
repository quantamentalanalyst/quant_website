abstract: "Ask whether sentiment predicts equity returns and the data answers with a question: which sentiment? Five proxies across three families — survey (Michigan), options (VIX), credit (Baa−Aaa), financial conditions (NFCI risk), news (policy uncertainty) — currently disagree to a historic degree: credit sits at its tightest reading since 2000 while the Michigan survey prints a record low, 47 points below what its own fundamentals model says it should (−4.2σ, the widest unexplained gloom on record). Measured with real-time standardization, overlap-corrected samples and HAC errors, the level of a fear composite carries no information about the mean forward 3-month return (Q5−Q1 = +1.3pp, bootstrap CI [−3.8, +6.4]; out-of-sample R² < 0). What fear prices is the shape: a 1σ rise adds +4.0pp to the 90th percentile of the forward-return distribution while the 10th percentile, if anything, worsens. The only mean effect that survives a five-way horse race is news-based policy uncertainty (+1.5pp per σ, HAC t = 3.6). The tradeable content is cross-sectional — cyclicals-over-defensives pays +2.1% per quarter in the fear tercile — not index timing: a fear-scaled overlay underperforms its own static benchmark (Sharpe 0.68 vs 0.77), exactly as the distributional result predicts."
